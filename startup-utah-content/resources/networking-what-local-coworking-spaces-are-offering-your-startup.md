# Networking: What Local Coworking Spaces Are Offering Your Startup

Source: <https://startup.utah.gov/networking-what-local-coworking-spaces-are-offering-your-startup/>

In business, networking isn’t just a tool — it’s a necessity. Whether it’s gathering support from fellow entrepreneurs, fiscal backing from investors, or a devoted customer base, growing your network can be the key to success.

Fortunately for Utah’s founders, the state offers a wide range of coworking hubs and business incubators that provide both resources to grow and opportunities to connect. With varying amenities and locations, here are a few standout spaces where startups can thrive.

[Kiln](https://startup.utah.gov/more-than-an-office-how-kiln-elevates-work-and-well-being/) is a leading coworking environment in Utah, offering startups a place to grow alongside like-minded innovators. With dozens of amenity-rich locations across the western U.S., Kiln’s flexible workspaces foster collaboration, creativity, and connection. Members can tap into a broad network of peers, mentors, and industry experts — what Kiln calls “a living library” for fast problem-solving. Its dynamic event calendar includes pitch nights, networking socials, wellness classes, and investor meetups, helping founders form meaningful relationships and build lasting professional connections.

[iHub](https://startup.utah.gov/ihub-utah-empowering-the-next-generation-of-entrepreneurs/) is more than a workspace — it’s a collaborative community where early-stage founders grow their networks and accelerate their ventures. Entrepreneurs work alongside experienced mentors and fellow business leaders, gaining hands-on support in areas like fundraising, legal strategy, and business development. With access to free office space, capital resources, and regular networking events, iHub fosters peer support and long-term growth.

[The Mill](https://startup.utah.gov/growing-utahs-next-generation-of-businesses-at-the-mill/) at Salt Lake Community College serves as a powerful networking hub and incubator, offering 24/7 access to coworking areas, private offices, and shared amenities. Startups can benefit from workshops and events designed to connect them with advisors, investors, and collaborators. On-site programs like the [Salt Lake SBDC](https://themillatslcc.com/resource/small-business-development-center/) and [Goldman Sachs 10,000 Small Businesses](https://themillatslcc.com/education/goldman-sachs-10000-small-businesses/) add even more support for companies ready to grow.

These are just a few of the many coworking options available across Utah. For those in more [rural environments](https://startup.utah.gov/how-a-statewide-coalition-is-fueling-entrepreneurship-in-rural-utah/), local [chambers of commerce](https://startup.utah.gov/how-local-chambers-help-small-businesses-thrive-in-utah/) help fill in the gap, offering not only workspaces — such as [San Juan’s business hubs](https://startup.utah.gov/san-juan-chambers-revival-marks-a-turning-point-in-rural-utah/) — but also serve as valuable connectors to help grow founder networks.

Looking for additional resources to help grow your network and strengthen your business? Visit the Startup State Initiative’s [resource page](https://startup.utah.gov/resources/) to discover additional tools, programs, and communities, or learn how to [start your business](https://startup.utah.gov/entrepreneur-journey/) in Utah.
